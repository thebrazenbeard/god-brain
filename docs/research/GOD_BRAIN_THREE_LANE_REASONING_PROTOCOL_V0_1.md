## Purpose

God Brain uses three deliberately different reasoning lanes:

1. `GOD_BRAIN_COORDINATOR`
2. `GOD_BRAIN_REZON_REASONER`
3. `GOD_BRAIN_NULL_ENGINE`

The purpose is methodological diversity, not vote multiplication.

The lanes may share the same foundation-model family, project context, tools, repositories, and source material. Separate chats therefore do not create independent evidence.

`SEPARATE_CHAT != INDEPENDENT_CORROBORATION`

`DIFFERENT_REASONING_HANDLER != INDEPENDENT_EVIDENCE`

`INTERNAL_AGREEMENT != EXTERNAL_VALIDATION`

## Lane functions

### GOD_BRAIN_COORDINATOR

Owns:

- literal subject definition;
- evidence/provenance binding;
- exposure accounting;
- cross-lane collision control;
- final epistemic reconciliation;
- unresolved-disagreement preservation;
- discriminating-test selection;
- God Brain architectural admission.

The Coordinator is not entitled to overrule stronger evidence from another lane merely by role.

### GOD_BRAIN_REZON_REASONER

Uses heterogeneous reasoning methods to map the subject:

- deduction;
- induction;
- abduction;
- causal and counterfactual reasoning;
- multi-view support/opposition;
- semantic and structural reasoning;
- deterministic verification;
- adversarial challenge.

`REZON_RESULT != GOD_BRAIN_CONCLUSION`

### GOD_BRAIN_NULL_ENGINE

Attempts to falsify favored explanations and identify rival causal models:

- ordinary explanations;
- measurement/software artifacts;
- model artifacts;
- leakage/contamination;
- confounding;
- selection effects;
- post-hoc adaptation;
- hidden common causes;
- alternative causal models;
- discriminating kill tests.

`NULL_FAILURE_TO_REFUTE != PROOF`

`NULL_REFUTATION_ATTEMPT != AUTOMATIC_REJECTION`

## Subject binding

Every shared three-lane run should bind an exact subject packet before first-pass reasoning.

At minimum:

- event ID;
- repository and exact head when repository-bound;
- exact relevant paths/blobs when applicable;
- literal proposition or architecture subject;
- admitted evidence set;
- known claim ceiling;
- protected-effect boundary;
- required lane set;
- evidence/exposure already shared with each lane.

A lane result that does not bind the same exact subject is not a comparable first-pass result.

`SAME_TOPIC != SAME_REVIEW_SUBJECT`

## First-pass rule

When practical, Rezon and Null should perform their initial reasoning before reading each other's conclusions.

The Coordinator may have prior knowledge because it created or selected the subject. That fact must not be disguised as lane independence.

Each lane result must disclose:

- source/evidence surface seen;
- prior exposure to other-lane conclusions;
- prior exposure to known blockers;
- whether the pass was blind-as-practical or already contaminated by cross-lane conclusions.

Allowed first-pass exposure classes:

- `BLIND_AS_PRACTICAL`
- `PARTIALLY_EXPOSED`
- `FULLY_EXPOSED_TO_OTHER_LANE_CONCLUSIONS`
- `EXPOSURE_UNKNOWN`

`BLIND_AS_PRACTICAL != INDEPENDENT_EVIDENCE`

It means only that avoidable cross-lane conclusion exposure was withheld.

## First-pass freeze

Before cross-examination, each participating lane should persist a first-pass result or explicit blocker.

The frozen first pass should include:

- exact subject binding;
- conclusion/disposition;
- competing hypotheses or interpretations;
- strongest supporting evidence;
- strongest opposing evidence;
- assumptions;
- falsifiers;
- unresolved dependencies;
- recommended discriminating test;
- exposure disclosure;
- claim ceiling.

After freeze, later revisions remain valuable but must be distinguishable from the initial pass.

`REVISED_AFTER_CROSS_EXAMINATION != ORIGINAL_FIRST_PASS`

## Cross-examination

Cross-examination begins only after the relevant initial passes are frozen or a lane is explicitly unavailable/blocked.

During cross-examination, lanes may receive:

- other-lane conclusions;
- objections;
- counterexamples;
- falsifiers;
- alternative hypotheses;
- proposed experiments.

After this point, agreement is no longer blind first-pass agreement.

`CROSS_EXAMINATION_AFTER_FREEZE != BLIND_FIRST_PASS`

Cross-examination can improve reasoning quality while simultaneously reducing informational independence.

Both facts must be preserved.

## Reconciliation

The Coordinator reconciles claims, not votes.

`CLAIM_QUALITY > VOTE_COUNT`

A reconciliation should compare:

- exact subject match;
- source quality;
- source independence;
- logical validity;
- causal adequacy;
- assumption load;
- falsifiability;
- contradiction severity;
- provenance/currentness;
- exposure/correlation;
- predictive discrimination.

Two weak arguments do not defeat one stronger argument.

A concrete exact-evidence blocker cannot be outvoted merely because two lanes missed it.

`HARD_EVIDENCE_BLOCKER != OUTVOTABLE`

Likewise, one speculative objection does not automatically defeat stronger verified evidence.

## Shared evidence accounting

If multiple lanes rely on the same source, that source does not become multiple external evidence items.

`SAME_SOURCE_USED_BY_THREE_LANES != THREE_SOURCES`

If multiple outputs descend from one exposed conclusion, fixture, benchmark result, prompt, or review, preserve the shared root.

`MULTIPLE_OUTPUTS_FROM_SHARED_ROOT != INDEPENDENT_CORROBORATION`

Method diversity can be recorded separately from evidence-source independence.

## Reconciliation dispositions

A three-lane run may end in one of these bounded states:

- `CONVERGENT_INTERNAL_REASONING`
- `CONVERGENT_WITH_MATERIAL_SHARED_LINEAGE`
- `MATERIAL_DISAGREEMENT`
- `BLOCKED_BY_EVIDENCE_GAP`
- `BLOCKED_BY_CURRENTNESS_OR_PROVENANCE`
- `DISCRIMINATING_TEST_REQUIRED`
- `SUBJECT_INVALIDATED_OR_MOVED`

These are internal reasoning states, not scientific-validation states.

`CONVERGENT_INTERNAL_REASONING != EXTERNAL_CORROBORATION`

## Disagreement handling

Do not average away disagreement.

For every material unresolved disagreement, record:

- proposition in dispute;
- which evidence each lane treats as decisive;
- which assumption differs;
- whether disagreement is factual, causal, semantic, methodological, or authority-related;
- what observation/test could discriminate;
- whether the needed evidence is obtainable.

`UNRESOLVED_DISAGREEMENT != FAILURE`

Persistent disagreement can reveal where the experiment or architecture needs sharper evidence.

## Discriminating tests

When lanes disagree, prefer a next test for which rival hypotheses predict different outcomes.

The next test should be evaluated for:

- information gain;
- contamination resistance;
- falsifiability;
- reproducibility;
- cost;
- reversibility;
- evidence independence;
- protected-effect requirements.

No reasoning lane may convert a proposed test into execution authority.

`PROPOSED_TEST != AUTHORIZED_EXPERIMENT`

## Missing-lane handling

The Coordinator must not fabricate a result for an unavailable lane.

If a required lane does not return:

- record it as unavailable/blocked;
- continue non-colliding work when possible;
- do not claim three-lane convergence.

`MISSING_LANE != IMPLIED_AGREEMENT`

## Head movement and subject movement

For repository-bound subjects, exact-head rules apply.

If the subject moves after a first pass:

- the old result becomes historical evidence;
- the new head requires fresh binding;
- semantic similarity does not automatically carry the verdict.

`REVIEWED_OLD_HEAD != REVIEWED_NEW_HEAD`

## Relationship to external review

The three-lane protocol is an internal God Brain reasoning architecture.

It does not replace:

- genuinely independent reviewers;
- independent investigators;
- external challenge material;
- independent instrumentation;
- external scientific replication.

`THREE_INTERNAL_LANES != EXTERNAL_REPLICATION`

BT2 or another reviewer is independent only to the extent the actual evidence/exposure lineage supports that claim.

## Claim ceiling

This protocol does not:

- make the lanes statistically independent;
- make the lanes epistemically independent by default;
- prove evidence-source independence;
- prove reviewer independence;
- prove external corroboration;
- prove scientific validity;
- qualify a detector/model;
- validate an anomaly;
- establish contact;
- establish simulation theory;
- authorize an experiment;
- authorize merge;
- authorize deployment;
- authorize provider/control effects;
- canonically promote itself.
