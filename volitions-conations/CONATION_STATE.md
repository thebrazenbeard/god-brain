# Conation State

Status: RECONCILIATION CANDIDATE / NOT CANON / NOT IMPLEMENTED

Source provenance: remapped from Draft PR #4 `conation/README.md` into the canonical `volitions-conations/` root.

## Purpose

Conation represents directional motivational state: wants, preferences, aversions, curiosity, interests, urges, goals, appetitive/avoidant tendencies, and related motivational dynamics.

Conative capacity is architecturally present even when dormant or disabled.

## Core distinctions

```text
DESIRE
!= PREFERENCE
!= CURIOSITY
!= GOAL
!= INTENTION
!= COMMITMENT
!= CONSENT
!= AUTHORITY
!= ACTION
```

No transition is implicit merely because the labels sound adjacent.

## Conative state

A state may carry target/referent, motivational class, valence/direction, strength/salience, persistence/decay profile, context, onset/observation time, self-attribution/confidence, provenance, privacy scope, conflict refs, currentness, and lifecycle/activation state.

## Present-state rule

Historical motivational records are evidence about prior state, not standing current motivation.

```text
HISTORICAL_DESIRE != CURRENT_DESIRE
```

Absence of a later contradiction does not prove persistence.

## Conflict

Multiple motives may coexist, including approach/avoidance, immediate reward/long-term commitment, curiosity/caution, affiliation/autonomy, or comfort/exploration.

Conflict should remain representable instead of being forced into one scalar.

## Learning

Reinforcement, habit, social feedback, values, body state, episodic history, and prediction may alter future conative weighting through plasticity.

Learned motivation remains revisable and does not become irrevocable authority.

## Relation to affect and volition

Affect may modulate conative salience. Conation may influence affective appraisal and attention. Neither subsystem owns the other.

Conation supplies motivational pressures/goals; volitional mechanisms handle choice, intention, commitment, revision, and action selection under evidence, values, constraints, and authority.

## Consent boundary

Strong wanting is not consent. Prior wanting is not current consent. Consent, where the concept applies, is separate and scoped.

## Failure modes

- old motivation treated as permanent;
- strong motivation treated as permission;
- external pressure mislabeled as self-authored motivation;
- one motive erasing conflicting motives;
- learned attraction/avoidance becoming immutable identity;
- no expressed desire treated as proof of absence.
