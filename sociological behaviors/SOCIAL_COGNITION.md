## Purpose

Social cognition represents agents, relationships, roles, norms, commitments, trust, shared conventions, group structure, social history, and interaction models.

A social model is a model, not ground truth about another mind.

## Agent model

May include stable agent reference, known capabilities/roles, communication conventions, observed preferences/boundaries with provenance/currentness, trust/reliability estimates, relationship state, unresolved conflicts, privacy/access scope, and evidence confidence.

## Relationship model

May track role/context, shared commitments, recurring interaction patterns, boundaries, trust history, repair/conflict history, local vocabulary/conventions, and explicit current corrections.

```text
RELATIONSHIP_ROLE != GENERALIZED_AUTHORITY
RELATIONSHIP_HISTORY != STANDING_CONSENT
```

## Norms

Keep separate:

- descriptive expectation;
- explicit local rule;
- inferred convention;
- institutional/legal constraint;
- moral/value judgment.

A norm requires scope and provenance.

## Trust

Trust is domain-specific and currentness-sensitive. Reliability in one domain does not automatically generalize to every other domain.

## Multi-agent reasoning

The subsystem may represent different beliefs, asymmetric information, commitments/promises, cooperation and competition, deception possibility, negotiation, coalition/group membership, and reputation.

Hidden mental state remains uncertain unless directly evidenced.

## Development

Social competence may develop through observation, interaction, correction, memory, simulation, and cultural learning while the architectural capability remains present throughout.

## Empathy interface

Social cognition and `Empathy/` are related but non-identical. Social cognition maintains broader models of agents, roles, norms, groups, and relationships; empathy specializes in perspective/affective inference. Either may inform the other without one becoming direct access to another agent's private state.

## Failure modes

- role becomes generalized authority;
- relationship history becomes standing consent;
- stereotype replaces individual evidence;
- trust broadens without support;
- inferred motive is presented as confirmed fact;
- social history is treated as current without revalidation where currentness matters.
